Feasibility & investment study · Ban Simuang, Vientiane · November 2026

Simuang Place

A 900 m² plot on Samsenthai Road, about 450 m from Vientiane Center and 250 m from Wat Si Muang. What to build on it, what it would earn, what can go wrong, and who to bring in.

The short answer

A 7-floor shaded building with green spaces, built in two phases. Vientiane rents can pay for 6–8 floors. Shading is set for the sun at 18° north; planting is placed where people use it.

  1. Phase 1 (now): a ground + 2 retail and food building: supermarket, coffee, restaurant, bank. About $1.7M, roughly $0.8M of your cash, about 15% return on that cash. Design the foundations and core to carry four more floors.
  2. Phase 2 (when proven): add two office floors and 30 serviced apartments to reach 7 floors (about 25 m roof, inside the 26 m cap), funded by a joint-venture partner or a serviced-apartment brand, so you put in land, not more cash.
  3. Fallback: a 30-year ground lease earns about $80k a year with no risk to you, but it is the lowest-value option.

Climate design

Shade every face, plant where people are

Vientiane sits at 18° north, so the sun passes overhead: north of the building at noon in May–July, south of it in winter, and low from the east and west every morning and afternoon. The plot also sits about 42° off north, so its four faces point north-east, south-east, south-west and north-west. Each face gets the shading its sun needs.

Green is used in a few places, not everywhere: a planted podium edge over the street, an indoor garden with trees on level 2 (fresh air for the offices), a planted top floor, a roof garden with trees in 1 m deep planters, a solar pergola (about 40 kWp) over the terrace, and room for rainwater tanks in the basement to irrigate the planters. This follows Singapore practice (Parkroyal Collection Pickering, Oasia Hotel), at Vientiane cost.

Added cost: about $280k for shading and green features across the full building (in the numbers below). Shading of this kind typically cuts air-conditioning load by 10–20%; no rent premium is assumed, though green space helps leasing.

Rule no. 1

Zoning decides the building

Vientiane's land-use plan sets height, plot coverage and floor area by zone. Which zone your plot is in decides everything else, and only the city's zoning certificate can confirm it.

Why the tower loses

A Vientiane hotel room at $60 a night and 65% occupancy earns about $4,400 a year after costs. Building that room costs about $77,000. That is under 6% before any loan, and the city just added 1,100 branded upscale rooms in 2024–25.

Why the low building wins

Street-front shops rent for $12–20/m² a month, the highest rent per square metre in the city, and they cost the least to build. Upper floors earn less and cost more (lifts, parking, fire systems), so each floor added dilutes the return.

What the land itself says

At $2,000/m², no use earns more than about 8% on the land's value. Prime Vientiane land is priced as a store of value. That is fine, but it means the decision is about adding income without risking the land.

Investors & partners

Who to approach, and with what

Match each target to the deal they actually do. Hotel brands rarely invest money; they bring a name and a management contract, and someone else funds the building. Approach in this order: tenants first (they make everything else bankable), then capital, then brands.

Ground-floor tenants · Phase 1

  • 7-Eleven Laos (CP ALL) — expanding across Vientiane since 2023; 100–200 m² corners.
  • Mini Big C (BJC) or E-Mart / No Brand — the supermarket anchor.
  • Café Amazon, Starbucks Laos (Maxim's), Joma, Sinouk — coffee frontage.
  • BCEL, LDB, JDB, ACLEDA — bank branch on level 1; banks are the steadiest tenants.

Joint-venture developers · Phase 2

  • Krittaphong Group — built Vientiane Center 500 m away; knows this catchment.
  • BIM Group (Vietnam) — built the Crowne Plaza hotel/office/retail complex on Samsenthai.
  • TK Group (owner of DoubleTree), TCR Group (Amari JV) — Lao hotel owners.
  • Haiwan, Qiancheng — Chinese developers building in central Vientiane now.

Brands for the upper floors

  • Ascott (Citadines / Somerset) — serviced apartments; already franchises in Vientiane. Best fit.
  • H World (JI, Orange) and Jin Jiang / RJJ — Chinese groups actively signing in Laos.
  • Wyndham (Ramada, TRYP), Accor (ibis Styles) — franchise small urban hotels.
  • ONYX (Shama, OZO), Minor (Oaks) — Thai operators with apartment brands.

Lenders & channels

  • BCEL, LaoVietBank (BIDV) — construction loans; BIDV lent $35M on a Vientiane hotel.
  • Investment Promotion Department (now under the Ministry of Finance) — official investor matching.
  • EuroCham Laos, LNCCI, Thai/Chinese/Japanese chambers — introductions.
  • Rentsbuy, ERA Laos — local brokers; C9 Hotelworks — hotel deal advisers.

Contact pages and recent deal sources for every name are in the research file. Opening dates for 2025–26 projects should be re-checked before outreach. I have not contacted anyone.

The next 90 days

What to do first

  1. Confirm the ground. Exact map pin, title deed, boundary survey, and a zoning certificate from Vientiane Capital Public Works & Transport. Nothing else is safe to spend on until the height limit is known.
  2. Test the tenants. One-page teaser to 7-Eleven, two coffee chains and two banks. Signed letters of intent at $14–20/m² turn this study into a bank-ready project.
  3. Price it for real. A local architect and quantity surveyor turn Phase 1 into a cost plan; get a soil test. Expect 3–9 months for permits.
  4. Line up money. Term sheet from BCEL or LaoVietBank for ~50% of Phase 1; your $0.8M covers the rest.
  5. Keep Phase 2 warm. Share the 3D model with Ascott, Krittaphong and one Chinese group; ask whether they want the top floors on a JV or a lease.

About these numbers. Laos has no published institutional market survey, so rents, construction costs, land prices and lending rates are estimates from listings and news reports dated 2024–2026. Land is valued at $2,000/m² (range $1,500–3,000). Build costs: $650/m² retail and office, $1,000/m² serviced apartments, $1,400/m² hotel with furnishings, plus 10% fees and 12% contingency. Rents grow 3% a year; exit value at a 10% yield after 20 years. This is a screening study, not a valuation; have a Lao lawyer, a licensed valuer and a quantity surveyor confirm it before you commit money.
